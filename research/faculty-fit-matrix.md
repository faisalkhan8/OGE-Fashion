# US PhD faculty-fit matrix: evidence-grounded recommendation research

**Prepared:** 2026-11-22  
**Status:** preliminary fit analysis, not confirmation of faculty availability or supervision.

## Research direction being developed

OGE-Fashion is being extended toward **evidence-grounded reasoning for faithful, constraint-aware recommendations**. The core question is whether an explicit ontology/knowledge graph and provenance-aware evidence layer can improve the faithfulness of recommendation explanations generated by language models, while respecting user preferences and hard constraints.

This is a proposal, not a completed method or proven novel contribution. The existing recommender benchmark is a small synthetic implementation study. It has not established real-user recommendation quality.

## Faculty matches

### 1. Professor Jure Leskovec — Stanford University, Computer Science

**Why the connection is substantive:** His official research profile describes applied machine learning on richly labelled relational structures, graphs and networks, with applications including commonsense reasoning and recommender systems. OGE-Fashion's ontology and provenance layer is a small domain-specific structured representation; a stronger future version could examine graph-based user/item representations and evidence-grounded recommendation.

- Faculty profile: https://cs.stanford.edu/people/jure-leskovec
- Research website: https://cs.stanford.edu/~jure/
- Research group: https://snap.stanford.edu/
- Application information: https://www.cs.stanford.edu/admissions/phd-admissions

**What to read before contacting or naming him in an application:** Recent papers from the SNAP group on graph representation learning, relational learning and recommender systems. Read full papers and identify one specific methodological issue that genuinely intersects with the proposed work. Do not rely only on the broad faculty bio.

**Potential adaptation:** Compare a graph/knowledge-aware recommender with non-graph baselines, then test whether explanation claims can be traced to user/item relations and evidence records.

### 2. Professor Thorsten Joachims — Cornell University, Computer Science and Information Science

**Why the connection is substantive:** His Cornell profile describes machine learning from human interaction, recommendation, information access, generative AI, counterfactual/causal inference, policy learning, learning to rank and implicit feedback. This aligns with the intended evaluation of user preferences, recommendation ranking and explanation grounding.

- Faculty profile: https://infosci.cornell.edu/people/thorsten-joachims
- Research website: https://www.cs.cornell.edu/people/tj/
- Cornell CS PhD application: https://www.cs.cornell.edu/phd-computer-science/apply

**What to read before applying:** Recent work on learning to rank, implicit feedback, counterfactual evaluation and human interaction. Select a specific problem that the current OGE-Fashion methodology can realistically study.

**Potential adaptation:** Evaluate recommendation and explanation faithfulness under preference feedback, missing evidence and counterfactual changes. This would require real or carefully governed benchmark data and matched baselines.

### 3. Professor James Caverlee — Texas A&M University, Computer Science and Engineering

**Why the connection is substantive:** His stated interests include recommender systems and LLMs. His co-authored 2025 paper, *ReasoningRec: Bridging Personalized Recommendations and Human-Interpretable Explanations through LLM Reasoning*, is a direct starting point for understanding LLM-based recommendation explanations.

- Faculty profile: https://engineering.tamu.edu/cse/profiles/jcaverlee.html
- Research website: https://people.engr.tamu.edu/caverlee/
- Prospective PhD guidance: https://people.engr.tamu.edu/caverlee/prospective.html
- ReasoningRec paper: https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-naacl.454/
- ReasoningRec code: https://github.com/millenniumbismay/reasoningrec

**What to do:** Read the full paper and code, document the task/data/model/evaluation setup, and identify one reproducible baseline or limitation. Do not claim to have reproduced it until a reproduction has been run and checked.

**Potential adaptation:** Compare LLM-only explanation generation with generation conditioned on structured, provenance-aware evidence, then evaluate claim-level support, contradiction and abstention.

## How to choose among them

The application should not list a professor merely because their webpage includes the word "AI." A credible fit requires:
1. reading at least one relevant full paper;
2. accurately summarising its research question and method;
3. identifying a concrete, non-trivial connection to OGE-Fashion;
4. explaining what is different about the proposed research;
5. checking whether the professor/group is currently accepting students, without assuming availability from a general profile.

The same base research question can be adapted to each programme, but the Statement of Purpose must not be a mass-produced copy. Cornell should emphasise recommendation, learning from interaction and evaluation; Stanford should emphasise graph/relational learning and recommendation; Texas A&M should emphasise LLM reasoning and evidence-grounded explanation.

## Integrity guardrails

- No professor has endorsed, supervised or collaborated on OGE-Fashion unless this is explicitly confirmed in writing.
- Faculty profiles do not establish that a professor has a funded opening for a particular cycle.
- Do not present the current synthetic benchmark as evidence about actual users.
- Do not claim novelty before a serious comparison against relevant prior work.
- Do not write or circulate a manuscript with unverified citations or invented results.
